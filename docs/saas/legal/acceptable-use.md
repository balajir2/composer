This Acceptable Use Policy ("**AUP**") describes prohibited and required uses of the Composer service ("**Service**") provided by {{LEGAL_ENTITY}}. The AUP is incorporated into the [Terms of Service](terms-of-service.md). Violations may result in suspension or termination of your access.

We focus the AUP on what genuinely matters to keep the Service safe and trustworthy for everyone. We don't enumerate every conceivable misuse; the categories below are illustrative, not exhaustive.

## Prohibited Uses

### 1. Illegal activity

You must not use the Service to do anything that violates applicable law, including:

- Activity that infringes on intellectual property, trade secrets, or privacy rights.
- Distributing malware, ransomware, or other harmful code.
- Activity that constitutes harassment, stalking, or threats.
- Producing or distributing child sexual abuse material (CSAM) or content sexually exploiting minors.
- Activity prohibited by export-control laws or sanctions regimes.
- Tax evasion, fraud, money laundering, or financing of designated terrorist organisations.

### 2. Harm to people

You must not use the Service to:

- Threaten, intimidate, or harass any individual.
- Generate disinformation campaigns, electoral manipulation content, or impersonation of real people without consent.
- Produce content sexualising or exploiting minors, or non-consensual intimate imagery of any person.
- Generate content that promotes self-harm, suicide, or eating disorders to vulnerable individuals.
- Generate medical, legal, or financial advice that you present as authoritative without appropriate disclaimers and qualified human review.

### 3. Harm to systems

You must not use the Service to:

- Probe, scan, or test the vulnerability of the Service or any other system without written authorisation.
- Attempt to circumvent authentication or authorisation controls in the Service.
- Disrupt the Service through denial-of-service, resource exhaustion, or similar attacks.
- Interfere with another customer's use of the Service.
- Reverse-engineer the Service for the purpose of building a competing offering, except where this right cannot be excluded by law.
- Use the HTTP node, code-execution sandbox, or other primitives to attack systems you do not own or have permission to test.

### 4. Resource abuse

You must not:

- Generate workloads designed primarily to consume capacity rather than produce useful output.
- Mine cryptocurrency or run blockchain validation through the Service.
- Use the Service as a generic compute platform unrelated to its workflow purpose.
- Bypass rate limits, plan quotas, or paywalls through technical means.
- Share account credentials or per-user API keys outside your organisation.

### 5. Misrepresentation

You must not:

- Misrepresent your affiliation with any person or organisation when using the Service.
- Use the Service to generate content claiming to be an authorised statement of {{LEGAL_ENTITY}} without our written consent.
- Pass off Composer's interface, branding, or output as your own without proper attribution where attribution is required by your contract.

### 6. Regulated content without authorisation

You must not use the Service to process the following data categories without prior written agreement:

- Protected Health Information (PHI) covered by HIPAA — {{LEGAL_ENTITY}} does not currently offer a HIPAA-covered tier on the managed service.
- Cardholder data covered by PCI DSS — Composer is not a PCI-compliant environment.
- Federal Tax Information (FTI) covered by IRS Publication 1075.
- Government-classified information at any level.
- Information subject to ITAR or EAR regimes without confirming compliance.

If you need to process any of these categories, contact us before doing so. Many can be accommodated through self-hosted deployments where you control the compliance posture.

## Required Uses

### 7. AI-generated output disclosure

When you publish content generated by the Service to end users, you must:

- Inform users that the content is AI-generated where reasonable to do so.
- Have human review for any output that materially affects an end user's rights, opportunities, or finances (legal advice, medical guidance, hiring decisions, credit decisions, etc.).
- Maintain audit logs sufficient to investigate user complaints about generated output.

### 8. Security hygiene

You must:

- Keep your account credentials secure and notify us promptly of any suspected compromise.
- Rotate per-user API keys when there's any reason to suspect they've been exposed.
- Apply security patches to self-hosted deployments within a reasonable window after release.
- Restrict admin role membership to the minimum number of authorised individuals.

### 9. Workflow safety

You must:

- Configure the `guardrails` node where appropriate for your use case (PII / moderation / jailbreak / hallucination classifiers exist for a reason).
- Use the `user-approval` node for workflows that make decisions affecting individuals' rights or opportunities.
- Apply size caps and rate limits sensibly to your published workflows so a runaway caller can't exhaust your quota or budget.
- Test workflows on non-production data before exposing them to production traffic.

## Investigation and Enforcement

### 10. Reporting

If you become aware of misuse — yours, another customer's, or a third party's — please report to {{ABUSE_CONTACT_EMAIL}}. We treat reports confidentially and will not retaliate against good-faith reporters.

### 11. Investigation

We may investigate suspected violations of this AUP. As part of an investigation, we may:

- Review logs of the affected workflows or executions.
- Request information from the customer being investigated.
- Suspend access to specific workflows, accounts, or features pending the outcome.

### 12. Response

If we determine a violation has occurred, our response may include any of:

- A warning with a request to cure within a stated period.
- Restriction of specific features or workflows.
- Suspension of the account.
- Termination of the agreement under the [Terms of Service](terms-of-service.md).
- Reporting to law enforcement where required or appropriate.

We aim to be proportionate: a first-time inadvertent violation typically gets a warning and a chance to cure. Egregious violations (CSAM, attacks on infrastructure, sanctions evasion) are immediate-termination matters with no warning.

### 13. Cooperation with law enforcement

Where we receive a valid legal process compelling us to retain or disclose customer data, we comply with the process while notifying the affected customer where legally permitted.
